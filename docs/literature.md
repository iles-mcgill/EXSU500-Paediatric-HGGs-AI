## Notes for the argument
- What works: U-Net-based models, especially nnU-Net and its ensembles, are the reference approach in paediatric brain tumour segmentation (2, 3, 4, 7). The whole tumour is segmented well: Dice about 0.83–0.91 (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9).
- What is missing: subregions (ET, NET, CC, ED) remain much harder, with lower and more variable Dice (2, 3, 7); many studies use small cohorts from few institutions (7, 8, 9, 13); models trained on adults perform poorly in children, so paediatric data or transfer learning are needed (2, 6, 7).
- Why it matters clinically: extent of resection is associated with survival in paediatric HGG (10), and tumour volume is used for treatment planning and response assessment (4).
- How our project addresses this: we use BraTS-PEDs (multi-institutional, paediatric HGG), with WT as the primary target, a patient-level split fixed before modelling, and a comparison of several models with uncertainty.
